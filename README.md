## 🌌 What Is Harmonoscope?

Harmonoscope is a living, breathing map of musical harmony — a visual observatory for anyone who has ever wondered *why* a chord progression makes them feel the way it does. Where a traditional music theory textbook hands you a static table of intervals and asks you to memorize it, Harmonoscope hands you a telescope and says: *look closer*.

Built as a spiritual successor to the ideas explored in Melodynator, Harmonoscope reimagines harmony visualization from the ground up. Every chord, every cadence, every borrowed tone from a parallel mode — all of it becomes an interactive object you can rotate, stack, and listen to in real time. The tool is designed for composers, students, teachers, hobbyist producers, and curious listeners who want to actually *see* what they are hearing.

This is not a cheat sheet. This is a playground for the inner ear.

## 🎯 Why Harmonoscope Exists

Many learners hit the same invisible wall. They can play scales. They can name intervals. But the moment someone says "this is a secondary dominant resolving deceptively into a borrowed chord," the thread snaps. Harmonoscope was born from the belief that harmony is fundamentally spatial — a landscape with hills, valleys, and shortcuts — and that showing it spatially makes it legible to anyone willing to look.

Whether you are charting functional harmony in the classical tradition, exploring modal interchange in modern film scoring, or simply trying to understand why the same four chords power a thousand songs, Harmonoscope gives you a cartographer's toolkit for the tonal world.

---

## 🔭 Core Concepts

Harmonoscope is organized around a few simple but powerful mental models:

1. **The Harmony Field** — every key is treated as a gravitational field with chords orbiting at predictable distances from the tonic.
2. **The Modal Prism** — modes are refractions of the same underlying scale, and Harmonoscope lets you slide between them to see which notes swap in and out.
3. **The Progression Trail** — as you build a chord sequence, Harmonoscope draws the path your harmony takes across the field, highlighting tension, release, and pivot points.
4. **The Function Wheel** — tonic, subdominant, and dominant relationships are rendered as a rotating wheel, so cadences feel like motion rather than memorized labels.
5. **The Voice-Leading Loom** — individual voices are woven as parallel strands, so you can watch how smoothly (or dramatically) each line moves.

## ✨ Feature Highlights

### 🖥️ Responsive Interface

Harmonoscope adapts gracefully to the canvas you give it — whether that is a 34-inch ultrawide monitor, a tablet propped on a music stand, or a phone tucked into a coat pocket during a commute. Layouts reflow, controls collapse into gesture-friendly clusters, and the harmony field maintains its proportions so a shape you learn on desktop remains recognizable on mobile. The interface respects your attention: nothing animates unless it needs to.

### 🌐 Multilingual Support

The vocabulary of music theory changes shape across cultures. Harmonoscope ships with interface and pedagogical content localized into English, Russian, German, Spanish, and Japanese, with terminology curated by native-speaking musicians rather than machine translation. Additional locales are welcomed through community contributions. Chord symbols remain universal, but explanations, tooltips, and tutorials speak your language.

### ☎️ Round-the-Clock Assistance

Questions do not wait for business hours, and neither do we. Harmonoscope maintains a continuous help presence through an in-app assistance panel, asynchronous discussion threads, and a knowledge base that grows alongside the project. Whether you are debugging a strange enharmonic spelling at 3 a.m. or asking which mode pairs best with a Lydian melody on a Sunday afternoon, someone — or something — is there to point you in a useful direction.

### 🗺️ Harmonic Cartography

Every key becomes a map. Harmonoscope renders the diatonic chords of a key as a set of interconnected nodes, each labeled with its Roman numeral function, quality, and common extensions. Click any node to hear it, drag it to a timeline, or hover to see which other chords it tends to move toward. The map is not static — modulate to a new key and the terrain redraws itself around the new tonic.

### 🎛️ Progression Sandbox

The sandbox is where ideas are born. Drop chords onto a sequencer-style timeline, reorder them with a drag, and let Harmonoscope analyze the result in real time. It will tell you the implied key, flag unusual resolutions, suggest smoother alternatives, and even propose substitutions drawn from the same functional family. Tempo, meter, and voicing density are all adjustable without leaving the view.

### 🧵 Voice-Leading Lens

Toggle the Voice-Leading Lens to strip away chord names and see only the individual melodic lines that connect them. Common tones glow steadily. Steps are drawn as clean arcs. Leaps flare with color intensity proportional to their size. This view is a lifesaver for arrangers who want to write choral or string parts that move with elegance rather than clunk.

### 🌈 Mode Colorizer

Modes are the same seven notes wearing different hats. Harmonoscope tints each scale degree according to its modal color — bright for Lydian's raised fourth, dusky for Aeolian's flat sixth, and so on. As you switch modes, the palette shifts, giving you an instant visual sense of which notes are characteristic. Students report that this single feature rewires how they hear modal music.

### 🔬 Interval X-Ray

Hover any two notes and the Interval X-Ray reveals the exact distance between them: semitones, ratio, common name, inversion, and a plain-language description of how the interval tends to feel in context. It is like having a patient theory tutor peering over your shoulder without ever making you feel small.

## 🏗️ Architecture Overview

Harmonoscope is engineered as a layered system, each layer independent enough to be tested and replaced in isolation.

- **Theory Kernel** — A deterministic engine written in Rust that handles scale generation, chord construction, functional analysis, and voice-leading computation. Compiled to WebAssembly for near-native responsiveness in the browser.
- **State Orchestrator** — A TypeScript store that manages the current key, mode, progression, voicing preferences, and user session. It emits typed events that any visualization layer can subscribe to.
- **Rendering Layer** — A hybrid canvas/SVG pipeline. Canvas handles the dynamic harmony field and animated trails; SVG handles crisp labels, tooltips, and accessibility anchors.
- **Audio Bridge** — A thin wrapper over the Web Audio API that schedules chord playback with sample-accurate timing and supports alternate tuning systems.
- **Localization Service** — A message catalog system with fallback chains, plural rules, and per-locale formatting for musical terminology.
- **Assistance Hub** — The connective tissue between the app and the support surface, providing contextual help anchors and feedback routing.

Each layer communicates through well-documented contracts, which makes it straightforward for contributors to add new visualizations or swap the theory engine for a custom implementation.

## 🎚️ Harmony Data Model

At the heart of Harmonoscope is a compact, expressive data model for representing harmonic content. A chord is not just a set of pitches — it is a labeled bundle of function, quality, inversion, and voicing intent. A progression is an ordered list of these bundles with optional metadata for rhythm, dynamics, and articulation.

The model embraces enharmonic nuance. A sharpened fourth and a flattened fifth are different objects internally, even if they sound identical, because spelling matters for readability and for certain analytical traditions. This distinction is invisible unless you ask for it, but it is always available.

## 🖌️ Visualization Layers

You can stack visualization layers independently, like transparencies on an overhead projector:

1. **Field Layer** — the base map of nodes and edges.
2. **Function Layer** — Roman numerals and functional color coding.
3. **Trail Layer** — the path traced by your current progression.
4. **Lens Layer** — voice-leading arcs and common-tone highlights.
5. **X-Ray Layer** — on-demand interval annotations.
6. **Annotation Layer** — your own notes, pinned to specific chords or moments.

Turning layers on and off lets you zoom from a beginner's overview to a specialist's deep dive without ever leaving the same screen.

## 🔊 Real-Time Audio Subsystem

Sound is not an afterthought in Harmonoscope. Chords are synthesized using a configurable voice engine that supports piano-like tones, warm pads, plucked strings, and neutral sine stacks for pure interval study. Latency is tuned for interactive play, and the scheduler compensates for jitter so that looping progressions stay rock-solid over long sessions. Alternate tunings, including just intonation for selected intervals, are available for users who want to explore harmony beyond equal temperament.

## ⌨️ Keyboard Shortcuts

- **Space** — play or pause the current progression.
- **Left / Right Arrow** — step backward or forward through chords.
- **Up / Down Arrow** — transpose the entire progression by a semitone.
- **M** — cycle through modes of the current key.
- **L** — toggle the Voice-Leading Lens.
- **X** — summon the Interval X-Ray.
- **H** — open the assistance panel.
- **?** — display the full shortcut reference.

Shortcuts are remappable through the settings panel and are documented in the in-app help.

## ♿ Accessibility Commitments

Music is for everyone, and so is Harmonoscope. The interface targets WCAG 2.2 AA conformance, with full keyboard navigability, screen-reader-friendly chord descriptions, high-contrast and reduced-motion themes, and color palettes that remain distinguishable for users with color vision differences. Audio cues complement visual feedback for users who rely on hearing over sight. Accessibility reports and suggestions are treated as first-class contributions.

## 🧑‍🏫 Learning Pathways

Harmonoscope includes three curated pathways to guide newcomers:

- **The Listener's Path** — start with no theory background. Learn to recognize tension and release by ear, then attach names to what you already feel.
- **The Player's Path** — designed for instrumentalists who can read music but want to understand why progressions work.
- **The Composer's Path** — for writers who want to expand their harmonic vocabulary with borrowed chords, modal interchange, and chromatic mediants.

Each pathway is a sequence of short, interactive lessons that build on one another, culminating in a sandbox challenge where you apply everything you have learned.

## ⚡ Performance Notes

Harmonoscope is designed to remain fluid even when rendering dense progressions with many layers active. The theory kernel is compiled ahead of time and cached, and the rendering pipeline batches updates to avoid layout thrash. On modest hardware, the field remains interactive at sixty frames per second for typical use. For extremely large analyses, a low-detail mode preserves responsiveness by simplifying edge rendering and deferring label layout.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need to read music to use Harmonoscope?**
No. The visual field and audio playback are designed to be approachable without notation literacy, though notation appears as an optional layer for those who want it.

**Does Harmonoscope work offline?**
Yes, after the initial load, most functionality runs entirely in the browser with no network dependency.

**Can I use it on a tablet or phone?**
Yes. The interface is fully responsive and touch-friendly.

**Is my data private?**
Progressions and notes are stored locally by default. Nothing is transmitted unless you explicitly opt into a cloud feature.

**Can I contribute translations?**
Absolutely. Locale work is one of the most valued contributions, and guidelines are available in the community documentation.
